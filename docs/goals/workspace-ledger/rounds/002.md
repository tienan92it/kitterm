## Effects

- **A day is not a key on disk, and the reason is exact.** The file keys
  on the transcript, and a day is summed at serve time, because a
  resumed session's bill is cumulative: its old share must come out
  before the new one goes in, and only a whole record can be replaced
  safely. `~/.kitterm/usage-daily.json`, version 1 like `push.json`,
  `0600`, 225 KB today.
- **"Never loses a day" is mechanical, not hopeful.** A refresh reads
  only transcripts whose size or mtime changed and replaces exactly
  those records. A record whose file is gone is never visited, so it
  stays, and nothing subtracts. A test holds a day older than every
  fixture across a refresh.
- **The day is the daemon's local zone**, written into the file and
  answered as `timeZone`. The foreman read the route and it returned
  `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh`, which is the point: a UTC bucket would put an
  evening's work on tomorrow.
- **The scan is bounded and measured.** One listing per project
  directory, one `stat` per file, plus each session's `subagents/*.jsonl`
  on the parent's bill. A full refresh over the real corpus — 779
  transcripts, 160 subagent files, 382 MB — takes **2.5 s**, down from
  17 s before a by-position parser and `memchr`. A no-change refresh is
  **19 ms**, listing and stats only. All on a utility queue, never the
  loop.
- **Two findings about the data itself.** One API request is written as
  several assistant lines sharing a `requestId`, so the reader counts
  each request once. And turns sum 2 to 6 percent under the bill's
  tokens, because Haiku title calls are not turns — so dollars come from
  the bill and turns only decide the day's share.
- visible: **the foreman read the route on a scratch daemon.** The last
  seven days: 10 Sep `$78.96`, 11 Sep `$99.95`, three silent days, 15 Sep
  `$66.93`, 16 Sep `$42.81`, each with its apportioned part beside it.
- persistent: fifteen files, two new sources, three new test files, four

## Reflection

The round was chartered to make history outlive retention, and the most
valuable thing it produced was a correction to a number that had already
shipped. `cost-per-round` round 2 tested its decoder against three real
transcript tails and all three had thinking counts; five of 118 did not,
and a $221 hole sat in the route for a day. Three fixtures are a sample.
A scan of everything is a census, and only the census found it.

The other keeper is the file's shape. The obvious design keys on the
day, and it is wrong: a resumed session's cumulative bill cannot be
corrected in a day-keyed file without knowing what that session already
contributed. Keying on the transcript and summing at serve time makes a
refresh idempotent, which is what "never loses a day" actually requires.
