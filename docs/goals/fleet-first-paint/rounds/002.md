## Effects

- behaviour: both routes answer at once. The body carries `pending`,
  `unread`, `readAt?` and `ageSeconds?`. A root with no answer, or an
  answer 300 s old or more, gets one read on `kitterm.yield`; a claimed
  key takes no second read. The routes read the cache and encode the
  body in the request's task, off the event loop. A range that ends one
  day later is served from the answer of the day before while its read
  runs. The page keeps "Reading the repositories…" while `unread` is
  above 0 and asks the yield route alone on each poll while `pending`.
- visible: the foreman ran the round's release build as a scratch daemon
  with a copy of the project list (9 repositories). Range "all", nothing
  kept: `/api/yield/daily` answered in 12 ms. After a restart the kept
  range answered in 4 ms with `pending: false`. A new range answered in
  0.9 ms with `unread: 9`, then `unread: 7` after 0.5 s, then complete
  after 6 s. The same range with `to` one day later answered with
  figures, `unread: 0` and `pending: true`. `GET /api/version` took 1.7
  ms at most while the reads ran. In a browser the waiting line showed
  for 2.3 s on a cold 30 day range and 3.0 s after a click on 90d.
  Tests: `RepositoryYieldPendingTests` (8) and
  `sessions-yield-pending-page.test.ts` (6).
- persistent: `Sources/KittermDaemon/RepositoryYield.swift`,
  `HTTPAPIHandler.swift`, `DaemonServer.swift`, `DaemonPaths.swift`
  (`yieldCacheFile`); `Web/terminal/src/sessions.ts`,
  `sessions-value.ts`; `docs/http-api.md`, `docs/architecture.md`,

## Reflection

The `PLAN` gate caught two faults before code: the plan encoded a 663 KB
body on the event loop, and every range that ends today lost its
figures at the first load of a day. The crew then found that the
foreman's day-roll rule did not cover the lifetime range, which moves
both bounds, and widened the rule for the daily route. A release build
of the product `kitterm` alone has no spawn helper beside it, so the
first bench run failed with "Socket is not connected"; `facts.md` gets
the fact. The 7, 30 and 90 day totals still wait once a day, because
their `from` moves; the page paints the rest, so the cost is one waiting
line.
