## Effects

- behaviour: the strip drops a proposal on a goal that is `done` or
  `stopped`. A goal that is `active`, `waiting`, or carries no status at
  all keeps its proposals in the strip.
- The crew settled the four questions:
  - **Which.** `LOOP.md` schedules a round only when no proposal blocks
    the next action. A `done` or a `stopped` goal has no next round, so
    nothing waits on its proposals. A `waiting` goal waits for the
    human's direction, and its proposals are the material for that
    direction, so they stay.
  - **Home.** The goal's own line. Inside the done fold each goal now
    reads `<title> N proposals record NNN`, and the count links its
    `STATE.md`, where the bullets live. A stopped goal's pending line and
    a dismissed item get the same link. The rule from capability 2 of
    `fleet-catch-up` holds: a proposal appears in the strip or on the
    line, never both.
  - **Count.** Unchanged, and it is `STATE.md`'s bullet count whole. The
    page keeps no per-proposal state, so "some still stand" is not a
    thing the page can know. A proposal stands until the human prunes its
    bullet at a direction check.
  - **Order.** Unchanged: approvals, needs-input, proposals, failed. A
    live proposal blocks a round and a failed shell blocks nothing, which
    is the order the title's own count already assumes.
- visible: a live check on a scratch daemon on 3971 with its own
  `KITTERM_STATE_DIR`, reading the real `docs/goals/` trees of `kitterm`
  and `market-data-pipeline`.

## Reflection

The defect was in front of the loop for four rounds and no check could
have caught it, because every test drove a fixture with two or three
goals and the page only fails with eleven. Round 5's live screenshot
found it in one look. The rule that follows, and that `facts.md` now
carries: a page that lists a repository's own records is photographed
against the real records at least once a goal, not only against a
fixture. A fixture proves the rule; the real tree proves the page.
