## Effects

- **The defect is the test's, and the crew proved the product keeps its
  promise.** `docs/architecture.md` writes the contract as: append to
  the ring first, then batch to the client; a client asking `since=n`
  receives every byte from `n`. `PtySession.attach` snapshots and
  subscribes under one lock on the loop, and the successor carries the
  whole ring, so no byte is dropped at the start or across the exec.
  In every forced run the reconnect replayed `cat`'s deferred echo,
  `resync` false, offset equal to the count.
- the window: between the ring's append of `cat`'s echo of
  `after-takeover` and the client's receipt of that frame. The test
  waited for **one** `after-takeover\r\n` — the tty's echo — then read
  the ring over HTTP while the second echo was still in the batcher.
  The ring led the stream by exactly those 16 bytes.
- forcing it open: the test's own `cat` cannot fail here, because both
  echoes arrive in one PTY read. The crew spread successive frames 250
  ms apart with a throwaway batcher and made `cat` echo 20 ms late:
  **10 of 10 red at lines 148 and 149, off by 16, before the fix; 10
  of 10 green after**, under the same forcing. The forcing was
  reverted; no runner branch was needed.
- the fix: the test waits for the stream to end with both echoes —
  `cat`'s is the session's last byte — with the mechanism in a comment.
  `docs/architecture.md` now says the ring leads the socket.
- counts: the plain case 50 of 50 under eight `yes` processes.
- persistent: `Tests/KittermDaemonTests/LiveTakeoverTests.swift`,
