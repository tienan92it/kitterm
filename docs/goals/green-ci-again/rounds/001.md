## Effects

- behaviour: **a product defect, not only a flake.** The wrapper
  `kitterm statusline install` writes hands stdin to the previous
  statusline with `printf '%s' "$input" | <inner>` under
  `set -o pipefail`. An inner that exits before reading stdin makes the
  writer take `SIGPIPE`, and `pipefail` reports the writer's 141 as the
  wrapper's status. In the field, a statusline that fails to start or is
  `command not found` therefore reports 141 instead of its own status,
  and the reason is hidden from Claude Code. Fixed by dropping
  `pipefail`: `set -u` alone, so the status is the inner's.
- reproduction, both steps: the wrapper's exact pipeline in four
  parallel bash loops of 500 gave **17 exit-141 of 2000**; the case
  itself 50 times plain gave 50 passes, so plain repetition is not
  enough. On a runner branch the case 20 times in one job gave 19
  passes and 1 failure, the same assertion at the same line.
- visible: `testTheWrapperReportsTheInnerStatusWhenTheInnerReadsNoStdin`
  feeds 256 KiB of stdin so the writer blocks until the inner has
  exited: **141 every time before the fix, 0 after**, and an inner
  `exit 7` arrives as 7. The fixed case ran 50 of 50 locally under two
  3000-iteration bash loops, and **50 of 50 on the macOS runner**
  (run 35827914857).
- persistent: `Sources/KittermCLI/StatuslineCommand.swift`,
  `Tests/KittermCLITests/StatuslineInstallTests.swift`,
  `docs/architecture.md`. The runner branch and its workflow were
  deleted; no workflow file is on this branch.

## Why the fix is in the product, not the harness

The harness's stdin and stdout pipes are read to end of file and
cannot close early. The pipe that breaks is inside the script the
product writes, so the script owns it.
