## The hole the round found

**Behind `tailscale serve`, with `--lan` and no `--trusted-host`,
`GET /api/sessions` answered `200` with full grade and no token, to the
whole tailnet.** The proxy connects from loopback, and `--lan` waives the
Host check for a loopback peer, so the daemon reads the proxy as the
local human and grants unauthenticated full access. That defeats both the
token grades and `--agent-control`.

The foreman confirmed it in `AccessPolicy.decide`. With `trustedHosts`
empty, `viaTrustedHost` is false; `isLoopback(remote)` is true for the
proxy; `lanEnabled` skips the rejection check; the branch returns
`.allow(.full)`. The comment three lines above already states why
`--trusted-host` exists: without it "anyone who reaches the proxy would
inherit loopback's unauthenticated full access, and the proxy could not
be a boundary." The mechanism is right. The gap is that `--lan` bypasses
it when no trusted host is configured.

Measured in all three configurations: `200` and full access with `--lan`
and no `--trusted-host`; `403 non-loopback Host` with neither flag;
`403 missing or invalid token` with `--trusted-host`, with or without
`--lan`.

**Nothing is exposed today.** The foreman checked: the live daemon runs
`serve --port 3418 --agent-control --retain-logs` with no `--lan`,
`tailscale serve status` reports no serve config, and the tailnet address
does not answer on 3418.

## Gap

class: contract

evidence: two flags that are each correct alone combine into an
authorization bypass, and nothing in the daemon says so at startup. The
round closed it in documentation, which is all this capability's
authority allows. A daemon that refuses `--lan` without `--trusted-host`,
or warns loudly, would close it in code.

## Decision

done. Capability 1 is complete on `goals/secure-context` at `35cef92`.

propose (`Sources/`, outside this capability's authority: make the daemon

unauthenticated full access to anything that can reach a loopback proxy.
This is a product change, not a documentation one, and it belongs to the
human to schedule.)
